#office365
Since she has a Microsoft 365 E5 license, which **definitely includes access to the desktop versions of Word and Excel**, the "no access to desktop apps" message indicates an issue beyond just the license itself.

Here's a focused troubleshooting approach based on the assumption that the license is correct:

**Troubleshooting Steps (with an E5 License):**

1. **Verify Sign-In within the Desktop Apps:**
    
    - **Open Word or Excel.**
    - Go to **File > Account**.
    - **Check the "User Information" section.** Ensure she is signed in with the **correct work or school account** associated with the E5 license. The email address displayed should be her organizational account.
    - **Check the "Product Information" section.** Look for "Microsoft 365 Apps for enterprise" or a similar designation that confirms the desktop apps are part of the license. If it says something like "Microsoft Office Home and Business" or indicates a personal license, she might be signed in with the wrong account.
    - **Try Signing Out and Back In:** If the wrong account is signed in, or even if it looks correct, click "Sign out" and then sign back in using her organizational email address and password.
2. **Check the Microsoft 365 Portal for License Confirmation:**
    
    - Go to [office.com](https://office.com) and sign in with her organizational account.
    - Click on her profile picture or initials in the top right corner.
    - Select "View account" or "My account."
    - Navigate to the "Subscriptions" or "Licenses" section.
    - Confirm that her Microsoft 365 E5 license is listed there and that it includes "The latest desktop version of Office" or similar wording.
3. **Repair the Microsoft Office Installation:**
    
    - Sometimes, the Office installation can become corrupted, leading to licensing errors.
    - **Close all Office applications.**
    - **Open the Control Panel** (search for it in the Windows Start Menu).
    - Go to **Programs > Programs and Features** (or "Uninstall a program").
    - Find **Microsoft 365 Apps for enterprise** (or a similar entry) in the list.
    - Right-click on it and select **"Change."**
    - In the Microsoft Office setup window, choose **"Quick Repair"** and click "Repair." If that doesn't work, try **"Online Repair"** (this will take longer as it downloads files).
    - Restart her computer after the repair process is complete.
4. **Check for Conflicting Office Installations:**
    
    - Having multiple versions of Office (e.g., a trial version, an older standalone version) can cause conflicts.
    - Use the **Uninstall tool for Office** provided by Microsoft to completely remove all traces of Office from the computer and then reinstall Microsoft 365 Apps for enterprise from [office.com](https://office.com). You can find this tool by searching "uninstall Office automatically" on the Microsoft support website.
5. **Ensure the Computer Meets System Requirements:**
    
    - While likely not the issue with an E5 license, ensure her computer meets the minimum system requirements for running Microsoft 365 desktop apps.
6. **Check Date and Time Settings:**
    
    - Incorrect date and time settings on the computer can sometimes interfere with activation. Ensure the date and time are set correctly and synchronized.
7. **Firewall and Antivirus:**
    
    - In rare cases, a firewall or antivirus software might be blocking the Office applications from communicating with the licensing servers. Temporarily disable them (with caution) to see if that resolves the issue. If it does, you'll need to configure exceptions for Office.

**If none of these steps resolve the issue, the next step would be to contact Microsoft Support directly for further assistance with her specific account and installation.** They have tools to diagnose licensing issues in more detail.

Given that she has an E5 license, the problem is almost certainly related to sign-in, activation, or a corrupted installation. Start with verifying the sign-in within the apps and then try the repair option.
