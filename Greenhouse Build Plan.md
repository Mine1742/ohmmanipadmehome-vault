**Revision note:** the design changed since this plan was first written — wall heights dropped (12'/8' → 9'/6'), the single west canopy became two smaller 6'x6' canopies (west + east, one panel each instead of two stacked), and the build location is confirmed as Camp Springs, Prince George's County, MD. See [[Greenhouse Design]] and [[Solar Power - Site Layout]] for the full detail. Phase 0 and Phases 3/7 below are updated accordingly.

Rough schedule: still roughly ~10-12 working days of labor spread across ~2-3 calendar weeks — the shorter walls cut some framing/glazing time, but framing and mounting a second canopy adds it back. Permit lead time now needs to be budgeted more seriously than before (see Phase 0) since a permit is confirmed required, not just possible.

Phase 0 — Permitting Check (start this before anything else — confirmed required, not optional)

- [ ] **A building permit is required.** Prince George's County allows accessory structures under 150 sq ft without one; this build's combined footprint is 168 sq ft (96 greenhouse + 36 west canopy + 36 east canopy) — over the threshold. Confirmed via [[Greenhouse Design]], not an assumption this time.
- [ ] **Budget ~$880–$945 in permit fees** — now calculated directly from the actual DPIE fee schedule: Shed permit $352.62, Solar Panels (Roof-Mounted) building permit $251.90, Electrical Permit for the Solar PV System $177.10, plus an estimated ~$100–$165 for the general (non-solar) electrical work. Full breakdown in [[Greenhouse Cost Estimate]].
- [ ] File through the county's Momentum online portal (Building Alteration / Residential Exterior and Grading category, per their shed-permit process — confirm a greenhouse+solar combination files the same way)
- [ ] Confirm the planned concrete pier footings satisfy the county's "permanent foundation" requirement for structures over 150 sq ft
- [ ] **File a separate rooftop PV permit** for the solar canopies per Prince George's County DPIE's rooftop PV guidelines — site plan, rapid-shutdown provisions, disconnect means, and grounding/mounting details all need to be in that application
- [ ] **SolarAPP+ correction:** confirmed this requires a Maryland-licensed contractor to submit the application (upload of a contractor license is part of registration) — it is **not available for a self-installed DIY system**. If this stays fully DIY, plan for the standard (slower, manually-reviewed) rooftop PV permit process instead; SolarAPP+ only becomes relevant if a licensed solar contractor gets involved
- [ ] Confirm setback requirements from property lines for the actual Camp Springs site
- [ ] Get approval on both permits before digging — budget real lead time here now that a permit is confirmed necessary, not just possible

Phase 1 — Site Prep & Layout

- [ ] Mark the greenhouse's 4 corners (12'x8') and the canopy's 4 corners (6'x8', attached to the west side) with stakes and string line
- [ ] Check squareness with diagonal corner-to-corner measurements (equal diagonals = square)
- [ ] Level and tamp the ground across the full footprint
- [ ] Mark individual footing/pier locations along the perimeter and at load-bearing points (canopy posts especially, since they carry roof + panel load with no walls to help distribute it)

**Tools:** tape measure, stakes, string line, line/laser level, shovel, tamper

Phase 2 — Footings (Concrete Piers)

- [ ] Dig post holes at each marked footing location, below Maryland's frost line (~24"-30")
- [ ] Set concrete piers/post-base brackets, checking level and height consistency across all piers before pouring
- [ ] Pour and cure — **budget 2-3 days minimum cure time before loading any framing on top**, longer with standard-set concrete vs. quick-set mix
- [ ] Pay particular attention to the canopy's posts here — they're the sole support for that section (no walls to share the load), so footing quality matters more there than on the enclosed greenhouse side

**Tools:** post-hole digger or auger, concrete mix, post-base/pier brackets, level, wheelbarrow or mixing tub

Phase 3 — Framing (greenhouse walls + both canopies' posts, continuous build)

- [ ] Bolt sill plates/posts to the cured concrete piers
- [ ] Frame the greenhouse's 4 walls: solid stud-wall construction on the north (9' tall) wall, standard framing on the south (6'), east, and west walls (east and west walls are shared with the two canopy attachment points)
- [ ] Set **both canopies' posts** — west and east, each running 9' at the north end down to 6.75' at their south edge (2' short of the greenhouse's own 6' south wall, since each canopy only covers 6' of the greenhouse's 8' depth — see [[Greenhouse Design]] for the geometry)
- [ ] Frame the rafters across all three sections (greenhouse + west canopy + east canopy) at the shared ~4.5:12 pitch — the "one continuous roofline" design now pays off twice, since both canopies' rafters are direct extensions of the greenhouse's
- [ ] Check squareness and plumb throughout before moving to roof decking

**Tools:** circular saw, drill/driver, framing square, level, structural screws or joist hangers, ladder, safety glasses/gloves

Phase 4 — Roof Decking

- [ ] Install roof decking/purlins across the full roof span — the glazed greenhouse section plus **both** open canopy sections — each canopy's decking needs to be solid enough to carry its own solar rail/panel load in addition to its structural role
- [ ] Confirm decking is flush and continuous across both greenhouse/canopy seams before glazing or panel mounting starts

**Tools:** plywood/OSB or purlin material per final decking choice, roofing screws, circular saw

Phase 5 — Glazing & Solid Wall

- [ ] Install single-wall polycarbonate on the south wall, both end (east/west) walls, and the roof deck over the greenhouse section — lay panels horizontally without overlap, joints closed with H-profile or aluminum capping strips, fastened with rubber-washered screws to prevent cracking
- [ ] Sheath and insulate the solid north wall (batt or rigid foam insulation between studs, exterior sheathing/siding) — this wall stays unglazed by design
- [ ] Leave **both** canopies' roof decks unglazed — they stay open below and get panels, not polycarbonate

**Tools:** polycarbonate panels, H-profile/capping strips, tek screws with rubber washers, drill (polycarbonate-rated bit), silicone sealant compatible with polycarbonate

Phase 6 — Doors & Vents (mechanical only)

- [ ] Hang the door(s)
- [ ] Mount the louvered vent door hardware and actuator motors in place — **mechanical mounting only at this stage**, don't wire them in yet (that happens in Phase 8/9 alongside the rest of the electrical system)

**Tools:** door hardware, vent door units + actuator motors, drill/driver, mounting screws

Phase 7 — Solar Panel Mechanical Mount (now two separate single-panel installs)

- [ ] Install roof-mount rail attachments into **each canopy's** rafters/purlins (through-bolted or lag-screwed, not just surface-fastened — these roofs carry real wind/panel load)
- [ ] Bolt rails to the attachments on both canopies, leaving a small air gap underneath each panel for cooling
- [ ] Set 1 panel on the west canopy and 1 panel on the east canopy per the layout in [[Solar Power - Site Layout]] — each canopy fits exactly one ~400W panel now that the footprint shrank to 6'x6'
- [ ] Secure both panels with end and mid clamps, torqued to the panel manufacturer's spec (use a torque wrench, not an impact driver — over-tightening cracks panel glass/frames)
- [ ] **Install grounding lugs/bonding hardware at each panel as it goes on, before the rails are fully covered** — this is much harder to retrofit after the array is buttoned up. Verify continuity from panel frame to grounding conductor with a multimeter as you go, at both canopies
- [ ] No live wiring to the battery/controller yet — that's Phase 8

**Tools:** roof-mount rail kit, end/mid clamps, grounding lugs, torque wrench, lag bolts, multimeter, drill/driver

Phase 8 — Electrical Rough-In (licensed electrician checkpoint)

**Stop and get this reviewed before energizing anything.** [[Solar Power - System Design]] flagged this explicitly — the wiring plan there is a planning-level design, not a substitute for licensed review.

- [ ] Place and secure the battery enclosure (24V 200Ah LiFePO4), MPPT charge controller, inverter, and 24V-to-12V DC-DC converter per the layout in [[Solar Power - System Design]]
- [ ] Run PV wiring from **both** the west and east panels (series-wired together) down to a central PV disconnect/combiner — longer, split cable runs now that the array isn't co-located on one canopy
- [ ] Wire PV disconnect → MPPT controller → battery bank → main battery disconnect → busbar, with fusing/breakers at each stage per the system design spec
- [ ] Install rapid-shutdown-capable disconnects — confirm with the electrician whether Prince George's County's rooftop PV guidelines require one at each array (west and east) or one central point is sufficient
- [ ] Run the grounding/bonding system: both panel racks, charge controller, inverter chassis, and battery enclosure all bonded to a common ground rod
- [ ] **Have a licensed electrician review the full wiring before the system is energized for the first time** — this is the point in the sequence where that review belongs, not as a final afterthought once everything's already live
- [ ] Schedule the county inspection(s) tied to both the building permit and the rooftop PV permit filed in Phase 0

**Tools:** appropriately-rated cable (per system design spec), wire strippers/crimpers, conduit, junction boxes, breakers/fuses, grounding rod + clamp, multimeter, torque driver for lugs

Phase 9 — Equipment Install & Commissioning

- [ ] Mount and wire the 4 circulation fans
- [ ] Wire the 12V irrigation pump through the DC-DC converter (not tapped off a single battery in the pack — see [[Solar Power - System Design]] for why)
- [ ] Wire grow lights, ambient lighting, and the radio to the inverter's AC output (or directly to the DC bus if DC-native versions were chosen)
- [ ] Wire the louvered vent door actuators (motorized, confirmed in [[Solar Power - Concept]]) — this is where their mechanical mounting from Phase 6 gets connected to power/control
- [ ] Energize the system and test each load individually before running everything together
- [ ] Verify charge controller is properly charging the battery bank under real sun before calling this done

**Tools:** appropriate gauge wire per load, wire nuts/connectors, multimeter, basic hand tools

Phase 10 — Final Walkthrough

- [ ] Confirm both required county inspections (building permit + rooftop PV permit, Phase 0) are scheduled and passed
- [ ] Walk the full system: mechanical (doors, vents open/close correctly), electrical (all loads function, battery charges), structural (no racking, plumb walls, secure footings)
- [ ] Move on to [[Greenhouse Growing Guide]] for operation once the structure and power are both live
