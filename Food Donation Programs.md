[[Food Access Hub]]

Where and how to route homegrown produce (or volunteer labor) toward people who need it, local to Camp Springs / Prince George's County, MD and the wider DC metro area.

Grow-and-Donate Programs (built for home gardeners)

- **AmpleHarvest.org** — national directory of 8,500+ registered food pantries that accept surplus garden produce. Enter a zip code, get the nearest pantry that's opted in to receive donations. Lowest-friction option if you just have extra from a home plot. ampleharvest.org
- **Capital Area Food Bank — Grow a Row** — connects DC/MD/VA home gardeners with CAFB partner organizations near them to form ongoing "produce partnerships," regardless of donation size. Contact garden@capitalareafoodbank.org. Also runs a half-acre Urban Demonstration Garden (produces 2,000+ lbs/year for education and distribution) with volunteer shifts March–November. capitalareafoodbank.org/what-we-do/farms-and-gardening
- **Plant a Row for the Hungry** — plant one extra row specifically earmarked for donation; pairs with local pantries. plantarowforthehungry.org

Local Food Pantries (Prince George's County) That Take Fresh Produce Drop-offs

- **College Park Community Food Bank** — 9704 Rhode Island Ave; distributes Saturdays 9:30–11:00am, mix of fresh produce and shelf-stable.
- **Bowie Interfaith Pantry and Emergency Aid Fund** — pickup Mon/Wed/Fri 9–11am; donation drop-off Mon/Wed/Fri from 8am.
- **Community Outreach and Development CDC – Labor of Love** (Capitol Heights) — food pantry open Mon–Fri, distributes fresh produce a few times a month.
- **PG County Dept. of Social Services (DSS) pantries** — permanent pantries at DSS service centers, free food incl. fresh produce, no registration required, Mon–Fri 8am–4:30pm.
- Full county list: [Food Assistance | Prince George's County](https://www.princegeorgescountymd.gov/1679/Food-Assistance) and [Food Pantry Services](https://www.princegeorgescountymd.gov/departments-offices/food-pantry-services)

Gleaning & Food Rescue (for larger volume / farm-scale surplus)

- **Prince George's County Food Rescue** (via Food Rescue US) — web/app-based platform matching surplus food donors with local social service agencies for pickup/transfer. 1401 Mercantile Lane, Suite 481, Largo, MD 20774.
- **Community Food Rescue** — Montgomery County's coordinated food recovery network (adjacent county, may still be relevant for growing sites near the county line). 9311 Gaither Rd, Gaithersburg, MD.
- **Celestial Manna** — faith-based nonprofit, gleaning/food rescue serving DC, MD, and Northern VA, supports 20,000+ people/month. PO Box 5857, Derwood, MD 20855.
- Tax note: Manna Food Center, Community Food Rescue, and Capital Area Food Bank are Certified Tax Credit Administrators — donating farmers/growers may be able to claim a MD food donation tax credit through them.
- Full directory: [National Gleaning Project — Maryland](https://nationalgleaningproject.org/gleaning-map/states/maryland/?fwp_state=md)

Community Gardens & Growing Sites (grow here, some let you keep or donate what you grow)

- **M-NCPPC Community Garden Plots** — county-run plots (6 locations); PG or Montgomery County resident, age 21+. Waitlist only as of research date. Contact park-permits@pgparks.com / 301-699-2400.
- **Prince George's Community College Community Garden** — produced 400+ lbs in one year; volunteers who help maintain it can take home produce. Contact: PGCC Office of Service Learning.
- **ECO City Farms** — nonprofit urban farm network (Edmonston Farm, Bladensburg Farm, Urban Farm Incubator at Watkins Regional Park); chemical-free/permaculture methods, farm shares + farmers markets, plus volunteer days and farm tours. 6010 Taylor Road, Riverdale, MD 20737. info@ecocityfarms.org
- PG County's Office of Food Security maintains a map of 30+ community gardens countywide — worth requesting directly since it isn't consistently public-indexed.

Local Coordination / Policy Body

- **Prince George's County Food Equity Council (pgcfec.org)** — county food policy council; tracks food assistance provider networks, urban ag planning, and food-insecurity initiatives. Good first call to find out which pantries currently have the most acute fresh-produce need.
