# ➗ Understanding Cosine Similarity

**Cosine Similarity** is one of the most common distance metrics in vector databases, especially for text embeddings. It measures the **angle** between two vectors, not their magnitude.

## 🔍 Formula:

\[
\cos(\theta) = \frac{A \cdot B}{\|A\| \|B\|}
\]

Where:
- \( A \) and \( B \) are vectors
- \( \cdot \) represents the dot product
- \( \|A\| \) and \( \|B\| \) are the magnitudes (lengths) of the vectors

## ✅ Key Characteristics:
- Ranges from **-1 to 1**
    - **1.0:** vectors are identical in direction
    - **0.0:** vectors are orthogonal (no similarity)
    - **-1.0:** vectors point in opposite directions (in practice, embeddings are rarely negative)
- Focuses on **orientation**, not length
- Robust when vectors are normalized

## 💡 Why it's preferred in NLP:
- Text embeddings generated by models like `SentenceTransformer` are often normalized.
- Helps identify semantic similarity even when the magnitude of the vectors differs.

## 🧑‍💻 Example:

```python
from sklearn.metrics.pairwise import cosine_similarity
import numpy as np

# Example vectors
vec1 = np.array([1, 2, 3])
vec2 = np.array([4, 5, 6])

similarity = cosine_similarity([vec1], [vec2])
print(similarity[0][0])  # Example output: 0.9746
```

## ⚙️ Common Libraries that Support Cosine Similarity:
- `scikit-learn`
- `scipy.spatial.distance`
- Built-in to many vector DBs like Pinecone, Qdrant, and Weaviate.
