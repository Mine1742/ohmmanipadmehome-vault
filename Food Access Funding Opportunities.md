[[Food Access Hub]]

Funding for starting or expanding a food-growing operation intended (in whole or part) for donation, local to Camp Springs / Prince George's County, MD, plus the state and federal programs that reach it. Deadlines below are as of the research date (2026-08-05) — verify current cycle dates before applying, most of these run annually.

Federal (USDA)

- **USDA Urban Agriculture and Innovative Production (UAIP) Grants** — NIFA competitive grant, two tracks: Planning grants up to $50,000, Implementation grants up to $250,000. Funds urban farms, community gardens, rooftop/vertical growing, and related innovative production. usda.gov/farming-and-ranching/agricultural-education-and-outreach/urban-agriculture-and-innovative-production
- **USDA NIFA Community Food Projects Grant Program** — smaller, nationally competitive (~$4.8M total FY2026), aimed at building local food-system self-reliance; typically requires 1:1 match and nonprofit status. Recent cycle deadline was mid-July — check NIFA's current solicitation for the next window.
- **USDA-NRCS cost-share programs** — soil/water conservation cost-share; urban growers commonly use the high tunnel (hoop house) cost-share option. Prince George's County is covered by the local NRCS field office.
- **USDA grant targeted at Black farmers / climate-smart urban ag** — up to $50,000 for existing farmers, up to $100,000 for new farmers, focused on climate-centric practices; part of a $3.25M USDA award specifically supporting PG County. Worth confirming current eligibility and application window directly with USDA.

State (Maryland Department of Agriculture)

- **MDA Urban Agriculture Grant Program** — open to individual growers, for-profit ag businesses, and nonprofits. Up to $10,000/application; covers capital improvements (refrigerated/dry storage, wash-and-pack stations, fencing, low tunnels, etc.). 2026 open-enrollment window was June 15–July 24 — annual, so watch for the 2027 window. Eligibility requires the operation to generate at least $1,000/year in sales *or donations* (donation-only operations qualify) and to be located in a Census-defined urban area — Camp Springs likely qualifies but confirm parcel-level status before applying. Contact: Bill Tharpe, Small Farm & Urban Agriculture Program, bill.tharpe@maryland.gov, 410-980-6160.
- **MDA Urban Agriculture Water and Power Infrastructure Grant** — up to $75,000/application, covers up to 87.5% of project cost, for water/electric infrastructure at urban farms and community gardens. State-funded through FY2027.

Regional / County

- **Southern Maryland Agricultural Development Commission (SMADC) Farmer Mini-Grants** — up to $2,000 matching grants; PG County recipients have used these for drip irrigation and equipment. smadc.com/farmer-resources/mini-grants
- **Chesapeake Bay Trust — Prince George's County Community Gardens Mini Grant** — $5,000 (gardens ≤400 sq ft) or $9,500 (gardens >400 sq ft); open to nonprofits, HOAs/community associations, faith-based groups, schools, and Green Teams in PG County. Match encouraged, not required. Rolling applications with preferred deadlines Aug 7 / Oct 9 / Jan 8 / Apr 9; apply ≥8 weeks before project start (no reimbursement for completed work). Contact Whitney Vong, wvong@cbtrust.org, 410-974-2941 ext. 122. cbtrust.org/grants/prince-georges-county-community-gardens
- **Prince George's County Urban Farm Incubator** (via ECO City Farms, Watkins Regional Park) — not a cash grant, but low-barrier land access + beginner farmer training, relevant if the operation is new. See [[Food Donation Programs]].

Financing (non-grant)

- **Foodshed Capital** — low-to-zero-interest loans, $5,000–$50,000, for agriculture producers; also helps with business plans, bookkeeping, and marketing. Useful once past pure-grant-eligible scale.

Fit Notes

- If the primary goal is donation rather than resale, lead with the MDA Urban Agriculture Grant (donations count toward the $1,000/year eligibility threshold) and the Chesapeake Bay Trust mini-grant (built for community-garden-scale, non-commercial projects).
- USDA UAIP and NIFA Community Food Projects are worth pursuing only if operating as or partnering with a nonprofit/institution — both petition-style applications favor organizations over individual growers.
- Cross-check zoning/urban-area eligibility against what's already confirmed for the property in [[Greenhouse Design]] and [[Solar Power - Site Layout]] (Camp Springs, Prince George's County, MD) before committing grant-application time.
